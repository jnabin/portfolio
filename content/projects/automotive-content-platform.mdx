## Context

A global automotive client needed one platform to manage vehicle content (manuals, guides, notifications) and deliver it consistently to every surface a driver touches: an administration panel for content teams, a mobile app, and the AI chatbot that answers questions from that content. This case study covers the platform side; the retrieval system has [its own case study](/projects/automotive-rag-chatbot).

<Callout title="My role">
  I lead the delivery team. I'm the primary engineer on the admin panel backend and frontend, sole author of the .NET 9 mobile BFF, and responsible for the messaging backbone that keeps every surface consistent with the content source of truth.
</Callout>

## Key decisions

**Modular monolith over microservices.** Eleven bounded services in one deployable: service boundaries are real, enforced by automated architecture tests that fail the build on illegal imports, but there's no distributed-system tax on a team of this size. The seams exist for the day extraction is worth it.

**Messages that cannot be lost.** Content changes publish through a transactional outbox: the event commits in the same PostgreSQL transaction as the data, then PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY pushes it to the dispatcher with a Quartz sweep as the fallback for anything missed. MassTransit carries it into Kafka. A crashed process or a dropped connection delays a message; it never silently loses one.

**One identity provider for every surface.** Keycloak OIDC fronts the admin panel, the BFF, and service-to-service calls. No per-app auth reinvention, and operator access is centrally revocable.

**A BFF, not a general API, for mobile.** The mobile app talks to a dedicated .NET 9 backend-for-frontend that aggregates and shapes platform data per screen, so mobile releases stop being hostage to platform API changes.

**Quality gates that bite.** 4,300+ backend test methods, including Testcontainers-backed integration suites and the architecture-boundary checks, with SonarQube on CI, plus a live contract test proving the content platform's output matches what the chatbot's ingestion expects.

## Latest additions (Sep – Oct 2026)

- **Website sources for the chatbot.** A new domain for brand and vehicle URLs, with a host allow-list, crawl runs and a "Run crawl now" button. It feeds the chatbot's [labelled web index](/projects/automotive-rag-chatbot) and is running on the dev server.
- **Each brand picks its AI.** OpenAI, Anthropic or Gemini per brand, with per-1M-token prices in the dropdown. The admin can see whether the chatbot accepted the choice.
- **Analytics that answer "what does AI cost us?"** Active users with a drill-down, and an estimated AI cost covering chat, weather tips and content embedding. Plus sortable question grids, per-variant ratings and feedback filters.
- **Safer admin work.** Smart delete for brands and regions created by mistake, dealer-locator links per region, and support-handoff settings with a verified recipient.
- **Privacy operations.** A GDPR erasure runbook across every service, and legal pages in 4 languages.

## Results

The platform feeds four surfaces from one source of truth, with content changes propagating through the outbox-to-Kafka pipeline and every service behind a single identity provider. The admin panel ships content-team workflows end to end, and the companion mobile app is delivered through the BFF under my team leadership.

## What I'd do differently

Build outbox observability sooner: dispatch lag, dead-letter visibility, and replay tooling. The pattern guarantees delivery, but operating it confidently requires seeing it, and we added that instrumentation later than we should have.
