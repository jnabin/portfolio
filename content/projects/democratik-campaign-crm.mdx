## Context

Democratik is the operating system for political campaigns in Quebec and wider Canada: door-to-door canvassing, predictive and auto-dialing with call logging, SMS and email campaigns, donations, surveys, volunteer coordination, and analytics. Campaign software has an unforgiving audience — volunteers with no training, candidates with no patience, and election deadlines that do not move.

<Callout title="My role">
  Second-highest contributor on the main Angular client (821 commits across a 1,100+ file codebase), working remotely with the Canadian team. Beyond the web CRM, I helped deliver the same product to four more surfaces — mobile, a Chrome extension, a Gmail add-on, and a self-service member portal — and guided 2 intern developers through code reviews and implementation.
</Callout>

## Key decisions

**Campaign automation as a visual canvas.** The journey builder let campaign staff drag and connect automation steps — triggers, waits, messages, tags — instead of filing requests to developers. Non-technical staff shipping their own automations was the feature that made the product sticky.

**Canvassing built as composition.** The door-to-door composer assembled surveys, questions, tags, phone actions, and donation asks into scripted field workflows a volunteer could follow on a phone at a stranger's doorstep.

**Territory management on real geospatial data.** Leaflet dot maps, heat maps, and hand-drawn polygons over a ten-thousand-point address dataset, with rule-driven coloring — turning voter files into something an organizer can actually reason about.

**A call center in the browser.** Plivo-powered VoIP with multi-party predictive dialing, do-not-call list management, and call-status tracking — no desk phones, no installed softphone, just the CRM tab.

**Bilingual as a first-class constraint.** Full EN/FR support with automated translation extraction — in Quebec politics, French is not a localization afterthought.

## Results

One product delivered across five surfaces: the web CRM, a Cordova mobile app with in-app calling, a Chrome extension surfacing contacts and tasks in the toolbar, a Gmail add-on matching open emails to CRM records, and a member portal handling memberships, renewals, and recurring donations through Stripe. Real-time chat and notifications ran over Pusher; configurable form and email builders, Avery label printing, and gamified goal dashboards rounded out the field toolkit.

## What I'd do differently

Establish a shared state-management architecture across surfaces earlier. Each surface solved caching and session state its own way; a common pattern from the start would have cut the duplication we later had to live with.
